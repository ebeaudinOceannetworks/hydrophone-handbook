Useful Equations

Sound speed

Sound speed in seawater is estimated from temperature \(T\), salinity \(S\), and depth \(z\) or pressure \(p\). These formulae support sound-speed profiles in Sound in the Ocean.

Mackenzie (1981)

A nine-term fit, convenient for a first estimate of \(c\) from a CTD cast:

\[ c(T,S,z) = a_1 + a_2 T + a_3 T^2 + a_4 T^3 + a_5(S-35) + a_6 z + a_7 z^2 + a_8 T (S-35) + a_9 T z^3 \]

where \(T\) is temperature (°C), \(S\) is salinity (psu), \(z\) is depth (m), and \(c\) is sound speed (m/s). Coefficients \(a_1 \ldots a_9\) are given in Mackenzie (1981). Typical range of validity: \(T\) 0–30 °C, \(S\) 30–40 psu, \(z\) 0–8000 m.

Del Grosso (1974)

Often preferred when a more accurate oceanographic sound speed is needed. It is a function of temperature, salinity, and pressure:

\[ c(T,S,p) = c_{000} + \Delta c_T + \Delta c_S + \Delta c_P + \Delta c_{STp} \]

where \(c_{000}\) is a reference sound speed and the \(\Delta c\) terms are polynomial corrections for temperature, salinity, pressure, and their cross terms. Full coefficients are given in Del Grosso (1974).

Snell’s law

A sound ray bends toward slower speed. If \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) are sound speed on either side of an interface (or of a gradual gradient) and \(\theta_1\), \(\theta_2\) are the angles from the vertical:

\[ \frac{\sin{\theta_1}}{\sin{\theta_2}} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} \]

Frequency is unchanged; wavelength adjusts so that \(c = f\lambda\). This is the geometry behind the SOFAR channel and surface ducts in Sound in the Ocean.

Acoustic wave equation

\[ \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = 0 \]

where \(p\) is acoustic pressure (deviation from ambient), \(c\) is sound speed (\(\approx 1500\) m/s in seawater), and \(t\) is time. Numerical models such as BELLHOP are discrete solutions of this equation for a given environment.

Sound pressure level

\[ \mathrm{SPL} = 20 \log_{10} \left(\frac{p}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)~\text{dB re 1}~\mu\text{Pa} \]

with \(p_{\mathrm{ref}} = 1~\mu\mathrm{Pa}\) underwater (20 µPa in air). A pressure \(10^6\) times the reference is \(120\) dB re 1 µPa.

Average Sound Pressure Level

\[ L_a = 10\cdot log\left(\frac{1}{n} ( 10^{0.1L_1} + 10^{0.1L_2} + ... + 10^{0.1L_n} )\right) \]

where

\(L_a\) = average sound pressure level (dB)

\(L_1..L_n\) = sound pressure level source 1..n (dB)

\(n\) = number of sources
